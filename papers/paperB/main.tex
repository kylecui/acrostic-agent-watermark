\title{Correlated Votes, Non-transferable Null Models, and the Template
Attack:\ Pitfalls of Statistical Watermarks on Text}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Statistical watermarks over text are deployed with detection rates
calibrated and quoted per domain. We show, with fixed-seed experiments on
an open-source watermark (AAWM v0.15.0), an independent reference
implementation, and the official PostMark implementation, that three
ordinary text phenomena break this contract. (1)~Repetition: deterministic
keyed word predicates make repeated words perfectly correlated votes, so an
unwatermarked form letter repeating one dictionary word 640 times scores
25.3 against a threshold of 8; per-word vote capping bounds the attack and,
at cap 3, is also the best in-domain operating point. (2)~Domain shift:
thresholds calibrated on one corpus transfer negatively---cross-domain
calibration raises false-positive rates from 0--6.9\% to 3.5--84\%, worse
than no calibration; in-domain calibration restores 0--7.7\% but fails when
the calibration corpus is too small. (3)~Templates: repeated-sentence
template texts not only trigger false existence verdicts but produce false
attribution to registered users (up to 46\%) that abstain-on-low-margin
protocols cannot prevent, because the signal genuinely aligns with a
candidate; mitigations rank calibration $>$ repetition prefiltering
$>$ none. Pitfall severity is statistic-dependent: PostMark's bounded
presence-ratio statistic is immune to the repetition and template attacks
and far more domain-stable (0.5--5\% cross-domain FPR), at the cost of the
multi-user decoding capacity $z$-test schemes provide. We release all
scripts, data, and the reproduction environment.
\end{abstract}
