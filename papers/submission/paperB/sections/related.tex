\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Lexical and post-hoc watermarks.}
Synonym-substitution watermarks predate LLMs \cite{topkara2006}; the modern
black-box line includes PostMark \cite{postmark} (presence ratio over
embedding-derived word lists), SimMark \cite{simmark} (inter-sentence embedding
similarity), and KGW-style lexical variants \cite{kgw}. Chinese
zero-watermarking \cite{wang2009} established frequency-statistics
registration. AAWM \cite{aawm} (our host) combines keyed greenlists with
multi-user band coding and an abstain protocol; none of the cited works
evaluates repetition constructions, cross-domain threshold transfer, or
template-class texts, which is precisely the gap this paper fills.

\paragraph{Statistical calibration for text watermarks.}
LLM watermark deployments increasingly report empirical calibration
(SynthID-Text \cite{synthid} reports production-scale thresholds). To our
knowledge no prior work measures \emph{cross-domain} transfer of empirical
null models; classifier-calibration folklore predicts miscalibration under
distribution shift, and we provide the first controlled measurement for
lexical watermark statistics.

\paragraph{Abstention and low-confidence protocols.}
Abstain-on-low-margin protocols are standard in watermark attribution
\cite{aawm,stealthink}. Our template attack is a constructive
counterexample in which real signal--candidate alignment produces high
margins, showing such protocols bound \emph{ambiguity}, not
\emph{truth-absence}; complementing guards (score-ratio floors) are
bypassable by construction.
